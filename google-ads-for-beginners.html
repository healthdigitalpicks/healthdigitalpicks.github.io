Google Ads for Beginners: Plan Your First Search Campaign

Smart Buying Guide · 6 min read

Most beginners open Google Ads, click a few buttons and spend money before they have a plan. A search campaign works much better when you plan it first. This guide gives you a simple order to follow: plan, check, evaluate.

1. Start with one clear goal

Before you touch any setting, decide what a good result looks like. Pick one goal only:

  • Sales of a product
  • Contact form or phone call requests
  • Newsletter or sign-up leads

One goal makes every later choice easier, from keywords to ad text to budget.

2. Choose your keywords

Search ads show when someone types a phrase that matches your keywords. Think about what your customer would actually type, not how you describe your product.

  • Write 10 to 20 phrases a real person would search.
  • Prefer specific phrases over very broad single words. Specific searches often come from people closer to a decision.
  • Learn the match types: broad, phrase and exact. Exact gives you the most control, broad gives you the most reach.
  • Add negative keywords for searches you do not want, so you do not pay for the wrong clicks.

3. Write ad copy that matches the search

Google search campaigns usually use responsive search ads, where you give several headlines and descriptions and Google tests combinations.

  • Use your main keyword in at least one headline.
  • Say what the person gets and what to do next.
  • Write several different headlines so Google has options to test.
  • Send people to a page that matches the ad. If the ad promises one thing, the page must show it.

4. Set a careful budget

Start small. A new campaign teaches you things, and learning should not cost a lot.

  • Choose a daily budget you are comfortable losing while you learn.
  • Do not raise the budget until you see the campaign works.
  • Check the search terms report to see what people actually searched, then add negative keywords.

5. Check and evaluate

Give the campaign some time before you judge it. Then look at a few numbers:

  • Click-through rate: shows whether your ad is attractive.
  • Conversions: shows whether visitors do what you wanted. Set up conversion tracking so you can see this.
  • Cost per conversion: shows whether the campaign is worth running.

Change one thing at a time. If you change everything at once, you will not know what helped.

Quick checklist
  • ☐ One clear goal
  • ☐ 10 to 20 real search phrases
  • ☐ Negative keywords added
  • ☐ Several headlines and descriptions
  • ☐ Small daily budget
  • ☐ Conversion tracking ready

Common beginner mistakes

  • Using only broad keywords and paying for unrelated clicks.
  • Sending visitors to a page that does not match the ad.
  • Starting without conversion tracking.
  • Changing many settings every day.
