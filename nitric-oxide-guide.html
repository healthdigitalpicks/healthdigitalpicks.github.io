Nitric Oxide: What It Is, Why It Matters and Which Foods Contain It

Discover how nitric oxide works in the body, explore foods that provide helpful nutrients and learn what to consider before choosing a supplement.

What Is Nitric Oxide?

Nitric oxide is a natural molecule produced by the human body. It acts as a signaling molecule and helps blood vessels relax. This process plays an important role in normal blood flow and cardiovascular function.

The body produces nitric oxide through different biological pathways. Certain foods provide nutrients that take part in these processes.

Why Is Nitric Oxide Important?

Nitric oxide helps regulate the function of blood vessels. It also takes part in several processes involving circulation and communication between cells.

Maintaining healthy habits, including regular physical activity and a balanced diet, supports overall wellness.

5 Foods That Provide Nitric Oxide-Related Nutrients

1. Beetroot

Beetroot contains natural dietary nitrates, which the body can use in the nitric oxide pathway.

2. Spinach

Spinach is a leafy green vegetable that provides dietary nitrates, vitamins and minerals.

3. Arugula

Arugula is another leafy green vegetable known for its naturally occurring dietary nitrates.

4. Lettuce

Lettuce can provide dietary nitrates as part of a varied vegetable-rich diet.

5. Watermelon

Watermelon contains L-citrulline, an amino acid involved in the body's nitric oxide production pathway.

What About Nitric Oxide Supplements?

Some dietary supplements are marketed to support nitric oxide production. Their formulas may contain ingredients such as beetroot, L-citrulline and other nutrients.

However, supplements are not a replacement for a balanced diet or medical treatment. Product formulas and evidence can vary. Always read the ingredient label and serving instructions.
